## 2. Model answer (teaching example)

Imposing higher taxes on sugary beverages and foodstuffs has increasingly been advocated as a fiscal intervention to curb poor dietary habits and combat lifestyle diseases. Although this policy can generate economic friction for lower-income households and provoke industry resistance, I believe that its long-term public health benefits significantly outweigh the associated drawbacks.

On the one hand, the primary advantage of increasing taxation on sugar lies in its capacity to counter widespread chronic illness and relieve public medical expenditure. Because sugar is an exceptionally cheap commodity that enhances the palatability of processed foods, food manufacturers routinely overutilize it, allowing added sugars to permeate virtually all sectors of modern diets. This pervasive accumulation of empty calories directly accelerates the incidence of severe health complications, ranging from adolescent obesity to type-2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease. When the retail price of sugar-dense products is elevated through taxation, consumers face tangible financial deterrence that disincentivizes impulsive purchases, ultimately improving nutritional choices and alleviating systemic financial strain on state healthcare systems.

On the other hand, the implementation of a blanket sugar tax carries notable disadvantages that cannot be dismissed. Such levies are inherently regressive, disproportionately penalizing low-income consumers who frequently rely on inexpensive, energy-dense staples that happen to contain trace sugars. In addition, food corporations may seek to circumvent the tax by replacing natural sugar with artificial sweeteners—substances that carry their own contentious health concerns—or simply pass the tax burden entirely onto consumers without modifying product formulas. Consequently, an improperly calibrated tax risks provoking broad public backlash while failing to foster authentic nutritional improvements.

In conclusion, after evaluating both perspectives, I am convinced that the preventative medical benefits of taxing sugar clearly surpass the drawbacks. However, to maximize its effectiveness, authorities must ensure that punitive tax rates are levied exclusively upon hyper-processed items and carbonated soft drinks, while exempting dietary necessities and channeling tax revenues into public nutrition campaigns.

## 3. Sentence-by-Sentence Breakdown & Analysis
- **Introduction**: Nominalizes the prompt (*Imposing higher taxes on sugary beverages and foodstuffs...*) and states an unambiguous evaluation (*significantly outweigh the associated drawbacks*).
- **Body 1 (Advantages)**: Identifies health preservation and expenditure relief; explains why producers overuse sugar; enumerates specific chronic outcomes; traces how price friction curbs purchasing.
- **Body 2 (Disadvantages)**: Details the regressive burden on budget-constrained families; explains artificial sweetener substitution and cost pass-throughs; warns of popular backlash.
- **Conclusion**: Reaffirms the superiority of benefits while offering a calibrated policy boundary (*hyper-processed items vs dietary necessities*).
