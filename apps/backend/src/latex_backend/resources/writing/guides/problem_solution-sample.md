---

## 2. Model answer (teaching example)

Across contemporary global industries, the widespread displacement of human laborers by automated machinery and intelligent computing has emerged as a defining economic transformation. This phenomenon is predominantly driven by commercial demands for unyielding productivity and precision, but its disruptive consequences on employment can be effectively mitigated through nationwide workforce re-skilling and progressive labor regulations.

The rapid migration toward workplace automation is primarily spurred by commercial incentives for operational optimization. Unlike human workers, who are susceptible to physical fatigue, psychological burnout, and scheduled periods of absence, automated machinery can operate unceasingly throughout the day and night without diminishing its operational efficiency. Furthermore, computer-controlled systems deliver immaculate precision, eliminating the manufacturing defects and clerical oversights that inevitably occur when humans perform repetitive duties. Although acquiring sophisticated technology demands substantial upfront financial investment, it drastically curtails ongoing wage liabilities and employee benefits over the long term, rendering automation an extraordinarily compelling financial decision for competitive corporations.

To counter the structural unemployment generated by this technological surge, a comprehensive policy framework must be mobilized. Firstly, governments should establish state-financed vocational retooling academies to retrain displaced workers, enabling them to transition into technical vocations that complement automation, such as robotics maintenance, data auditing, and automated logistics supervision. Concurrently, national education systems must recalibrate their curriculums away from manual and repetitive competencies, prioritizing uniquely human faculties—such as complex problem-solving, strategic creativity, and interpersonal negotiation—that machines cannot emulate. Finally, governments can institute transitional regulations requiring firms that implement large-scale automation to co-fund redeployment programs for their erstwhile workforce.

In conclusion, although the substitution of human labor by automated technology is an inevitable consequence of the pursuit of industrial efficiency, society is not powerless against its disruptive aftershocks. Through strategic public retraining initiatives and educational adaptation, modern economies can harness the power of automation while ensuring that the dignity and prosperity of human workers remain safeguarded.

## 3. Sentence-by-Sentence Breakdown & Analysis
- **Introduction**: Introduces the macro trend with high-level lexical precision (*widespread displacement*, *intelligent computing*, *defining economic transformation*) and establishes both causes and mitigation avenues.
- **Body 1 (Causes)**: Anchors on commercial optimization; contrasts human fatigue with continuous 24/7 uptime; identifies error elimination in repetitive duties; explains capital expenditure vs ongoing wage liabilities.
- **Body 2 (Solutions)**: Proposes state-financed vocational academies; educational reform emphasizing non-replicable human faculties; corporate transition levies.
- **Conclusion**: Balanced synthesis of technological progress and human worker safeguarding.
