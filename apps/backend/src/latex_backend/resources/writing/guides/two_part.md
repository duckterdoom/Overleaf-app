### Comprehensive Master Template and Structural Guide for IELTS Writing Task 2: Two-Part Question Essays

#### 1. Assessment Foundations and Strategic Importance of the Two-Part Question Essay

In the IELTS Academic and General Training Writing modules, Task 2 is the decisive component of the writing assessment, carrying **two-thirds (2/3) of the total Writing mark** . Candidates are tasked with composing a formal, fully developed academic essay within a strict 40-minute time allocation. While the official rubric mandates a minimum threshold of 250 words, senior examiners consistently target an optimal length of **270 to 280 words** . Producing fewer than 250 words triggers severe penalties under Task Achievement due to under-developed main ideas, whereas exceeding 300 words frequently induces structural degradation, increases grammatical vulnerability, and risks acute time management failure. Task 2 requires an authoritative academic register, logical paragraphing, and precise analytical reasoning across broad socio-cultural domains such as public health, technology, education, environmental governance, and economic development.

##### Evaluation Criteria Analysis

##### Deconstruction of the Two-Part Question Architecture

A Two-Part Question prompt presents an initial premise followed by two explicit, interconnected sub-questions. Unlike standard *Agree/Disagree* or *Discussion* prompts, Two-Part questions mandate distinct analytical operations. Common structural variations include:

* **Causes & Solutions:** Direct inquiries into underlying drivers and remedies (e.g., *Why is this happening, and what can be done to solve it?* ).
* **Why & How:** Structural probes examining societal factors and practical execution mechanisms (e.g., *Why do millions of tons of food go to waste, and how can society eliminate this problem?* ).
* **Reasons & Evaluation/Change:** Inquiries requesting explanatory factors alongside a normative judgment (e.g., *What are the reasons for gendered subject selection, and should this tendency be changed?* ).
* **Causes & Effects:** Analytical inquiries evaluating underlying drivers and their resulting societal or individual impacts (e.g., *Why do people engage in risky behaviors, and what consequences ensue?* ).

From an examiner’s perspective, failing to allocate equal analytical depth to both prompt components represents a failure in Task Achievement. If Body Paragraph 1 thoroughly explains two underlying causes, but Body Paragraph 2 offers only a single, under-developed recommendation, the essay exhibits a clear Task Achievement deficit. Achieving Band 8.0+ requires a balanced, dual-core architecture where both sub-questions receive equal structural real estate, rigorous cause-effect reasoning, and explicit exemplar support.

*Mastering these assessment mechanics provides the foundation for executing a disciplined pre-writing methodology prior to essay drafting.*

#### 2. Five-Step Pre-Writing Methodology and Prompt Analysis Framework

Under high-pressure exam conditions, candidates frequently experience off-topic drift, structural fragmentation, or logical collapse mid-essay. These failures stem almost universally from rushed or omitted pre-writing analysis. Implementing a rigorous 5-step pre-writing methodology ensures prompt coverage, precise structural balance, and lexical control before writing the first sentence.

+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

| 5-STEP PRE-WRITING METHODOLOGY |

+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

| 1. Prompt Analysis --> Isolate Main Keywords, Micro-keywords & Instructions |

| 2. Brainstorming --> Apply "Feasibility & Depth Filter" (Max 2 ideas/Q) |

| 3. Structural Mapping --> Allocate 4-Paragraph Architecture & Word Budget |

| 4. Lexical Selection --> Pre-select Synonyms, Collocations & Connectives |

| 5. Draft Execution --> Write to Target (270-280 Words) + 2-Min Proofread |

+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

##### Step-by-Step Execution Workflow

* **Prompt Analysis & Keyword Identification (Time: 2 Minutes):** Deconstruct the prompt into three distinct operational layers:
* *Main Keywords:* The macro-topic or broad thematic domain.
* *Micro-keywords:* The specific context, limitation, or qualifier constraining the prompt.
* *Instruction Words:* The two explicit sub-questions dictating the exact task requirements.
* **Brainstorming & Idea Selection — The Feasibility & Depth Filter (Time: 3 Minutes):** Generate potential responses for each sub-question and apply a strict **Feasibility & Depth Filter** : reject overly broad or abstract concepts that cannot be fully explained within a 2-sentence logical cause-and-effect chain under exam time constraints. Select **exactly two concrete supporting arguments per sub-question** . Listing three or more points invariably leads to superficial listing, resulting in under-developed main ideas.
* **Structural Outlining (Time: 2 Minutes):** Establish a strict paragraph allocation plan:
* *Introduction (40–50 words):* Paraphrased Context + Dual-Answer Thesis Statement.
* *Body Paragraph 1 (90–100 words):* Sub-Question 1 Focus (Topic Sentence + Dual Cause-Effect Chains + Concrete Example).
* *Body Paragraph 2 (90–100 words):* Sub-Question 2 Focus (Topic Sentence + Dual Mechanism-Impact Chains + Concrete Example).
* *Conclusion (30–40 words):* Restatement of Core Answers + Forward-Looking Outlook.
* **Lexical Preparation (Time: 2 Minutes):** Identify key prompt terms and list 2–3 academic synonyms, formal collocations, and transition markers to prevent word repetition and demonstrate Lexical Resource.
* **Drafting & Execution (Time: 31 Minutes):** Write the response following the planned outline, aiming for 270–280 words, reserving 2 minutes for final proofreading to eliminate minor grammatical or spelling errors.

##### Prompt Deconstruction Matrix

**Sample Prompt:** *"Every day, millions of tons of food are wasted all over the world. Why do you think this is happening? And how can we solve this problem?"*

1. **Main Keywords (Core Domain):** Global food waste / Resource management ( *food wastage, discarding agricultural output* ).
2. **Micro-keywords (Specific Focus):** Daily systemic waste on a global scale ( *millions of tons, every day, all over the world* ).
3. **Instruction Words (Task Demands):**
4. *Sub-Question 1 (Why):* Identify the primary drivers responsible for systemic food wastage.
5. *Sub-Question 2 (How):* Propose actionable, concrete measures to resolve the problem.

*With the prompt deconstructed and key arguments filtered for feasibility, the candidate must translate these elements into a standardized structural blueprint.*

##### Introduction Paragraph Architecture (2–3 Sentences | 40–50 Words)

* **Sentence 1 (Background Context / Paraphrase):** Rephrase the prompt context using academic synonyms and altered grammatical structures.
* *Formula:* In contemporary society, rephrased global phenomenon/issue has emerged as a pressingly prominent concern.
* **Sentences 2–3 (Thesis & Direct Answer Statement):** Explicitly present direct, high-level answers to both sub-questions. Avoid generic outline statements such as *"This essay will discuss the causes and solutions."*
* *Single Compound-Complex Option (Standard Prompts):* While Primary Driver/Reason for Q1 and Secondary Driver/Reason for Q1 largely account for this trend, it can be effectively mitigated through Primary Action/Solution for Q2 alongside Secondary Action/Solution for Q2.
* *Two-Sentence Option (Complex or Normative Prompts):* This phenomenon is primarily driven by Core Reason for Q1. Consequently, I firmly maintain that Direct Answer/Evaluation for Q2, e.g., this tendency ought to be altered through targeted policy intervention.

##### Body Paragraph 1 Blueprint: Addressing Sub-Question 1 (3–4 Sentences | 90–100 Words)

* **Sentence 1 (Topic Sentence):** State the main focus directly answering Sub-Question 1.
* *Formula (Causes/Reasons):* The principal factors driving Phenomenon are primarily attributable to Main Cause 1 and Main Cause 2.
* *Formula (Why Question):* First and foremost, Phenomenon occurs on the simple grounds that Primary Explanation/Driver.
* **Sentences 2–3 (Explanation & Cause-Effect Logic):** Deepen the rationale behind the primary point, demonstrating a clear logical chain without gaps.
* *Formula:* Because Mechanism of Cause 1, Immediate outcome. As a direct result, Secondary consequence that solidifies the impact.
* **Sentence 4 (Concrete Exemplification):** Provide a clear, plausible supporting example reinforcing the core argument.
* *Formula:* A prime example of this is Concrete case study/real-world context, which clearly illustrates Key analytical point of Body 1.

##### Body Paragraph 2 Blueprint: Addressing Sub-Question 2 (3–4 Sentences | 90–100 Words)

* **Sentence 1 (Topic Sentence):** Introduce a clear transitional marker and state the primary response answering Sub-Question 2.
* *Formula (Solutions/How):* To address this problem, targeted measures must be implemented, chief among which is Primary Solution/Action.
* *Formula (Necessity for Change/Evaluation):* Regarding whether this situation should be altered, there is an urgent necessity to change this trend through Action/Strategy.
* **Sentences 2–3 (Explanation & Impact Analysis):** Detail the mechanism through which the proposed measure operates to resolve the issue or effect change.
* *Formula:* By taking the bull by the horns and enforcing Specific Action, Target Entity/Government can Immediate mechanical effect. Consequently, Long-term societal/individual resolution.
* **Sentence 4 (Concrete Exemplification):** Supply an illustrative real-world or scenario-based example confirming the viability of the response.
* *Formula:* For instance, Specific country/policy initiative has successfully Achieved outcome, demonstrating the efficacy of this approach.

##### Conclusion Paragraph Blueprint (1–2 Sentences | 30–40 Words)

* **Sentence 1 (Restatement of Core Answers):** Summarize the central conclusions drawn for both sub-questions without introducing novel claims or peripheral ideas.
* *Formula:* In conclusion, while Phenomenon stems primarily from Summary of Cause 1 and Summary of Cause 2, it can be successfully resolved by Summary of Solution 1 and Summary of Solution 2.
* **Sentence 2 (Final Synthesis / Outlook):** Offer a definitive, forward-looking evaluation.
* *Formula:* Provided that these strategic interventions are sustained, Positive long-term projection/societal stability can be secured.

#### 4. Advanced Linguistic Execution and Band 8.0+ Language Strategies

Achieving Band 8.0+ in Grammatical Range and Accuracy (GRA) and Lexical Resource (LR) requires moving beyond basic syntax and generic vocabulary. Candidates must demonstrate flexible control of complex structures alongside topic-specific academic collocations.

##### Grammatical Complexity Toolkit

High-scoring responses feature a natural integration of complex, passive, and emphatic sentence structures:

* **Complex Subordination:** Link independent and dependent clauses using sophisticated conjunctions ( *although, whereas, provided that, in order that* ).
* *Usage Rule:* Maintain logical punctuation—subordinate clauses opening a sentence must be followed by a comma.
* *Band 8.0+ Model:* *"Although powerful reminders of sound lifestyle choices are readily available, many individuals remain in denial regarding their personal susceptibility to chronic illness."*
* **Agentless & Focused Passive Voice:** Use passive constructions to maintain an objective, academic register, directing focus onto phenomena, institutional policies, and societal outcomes rather than informal personal pronouns ( *I, You, We* ).
* *Usage Rule:* Combine auxiliary verbs with past participles (Subject + to be + Past Participle).
* *Band 8.0+ Model:* *"If fiscal penalties are enforced on high-fat food items, consumer habits can be shifted toward wholesome alternatives."*
* **Emphatic Inversion Structures:** Deploy restrictive inverted adverbials to demonstrate advanced control of word order and structural emphasis.
* *Usage Rule:* When initiating a clause with a restrictive negative adverbial (e.g., *Rarely, Not only, Under no circumstances* ), invert the auxiliary verb and subject.
* *Band 8.0+ Model:* *"Not only does economic growth increase consumer spending, but it also elevates the volume of municipal waste generated daily."*
* *Band 8.0+ Model:* *"Rarely do younger generations prioritize traditional linguistic heritage when exposed to globalized digital media early."*
* **Non-Defining Relative Clauses:** Embed non-essential descriptive context to elaborate on main nouns seamlessly.
* *Usage Rule:* Set off non-defining relative clauses using commas before *which* or *who* .
* *Band 8.0+ Model:* *"Local governments should invest in energy-efficient infrastructure, which significantly reduces urban dependence on fossil fuels."*

#### 5. High-Scoring Model Essay Deconstruction and Practical Application

##### Sample Prompt

**Scientists tell us that some activities are good for health and others are bad. Despite knowing that, millions of people still continue doing unhealthy activities. What are the causes and what are the solutions for this?**

*(Context Note: The original source text—Essay 26 in the reference repository—presented a truncated 200-word outline. The model essay below expands those core ideas into a 275-word Band 8.0+ essay by elaborating brief mentions of smoking and sedentary lifestyles into complete cause-effect-example chains.)*

##### Band 8.0+ Model Essay

Modern citizens are regularly informed about the severe dangers of leading unsound lifestyles. Despite spectacular advances in the medical field, millions of individuals continue to engage in health-damaging habits, giving rise to diseases that prey on the minds of populations worldwide. This persistent behavior is primarily attributable to undue optimism and the addictive nature of modern habits, but it can be effectively counteracted if governments take the bull by the horns through fiscal penalties and public awareness campaigns.

There are two primary causes responsible for the prevalence of self-destructive habits among the public. First and foremost, most individuals are prone to undue optimism regarding their physical well-being. Although powerful and constant reminders of what unsound lifestyles bring are readily available everywhere, people often remain in denial, convincing themselves that they will hardly be inflicted by severe illnesses. Secondly, once individuals succumb to unhealthy routines, breaking them proves exceptionally difficult due to physical or psychological dependency. For example, individuals with long-standing smoking habits find it nearly impossible to reduce their reliance on cigarettes due to high addictiveness, while those leading sedentary lifestyles struggle to engage in regular exercise simply because physical exertion is perceived as unpleasurable.

To address this issue, comprehensive solutions must be implemented by governing authorities. Chief among these measures is imposing higher taxes on unhealthy consumer products, such as foods containing high concentrations of fat, alcohol, and tobacco. This fiscal strategy promises a direct impact by raising the cost of self-indulgence, thereby deterring consumers from taking health choices for granted. Furthermore, healthy lifestyles should be actively promoted across mass media platforms, whereas self-destructive habits ought to be strictly criticized. By fostering a strong sense of self-protection within local communities, individuals will find it significantly easier to break their old routines and adopt wholesome living standards.

In conclusion, the practice of continuing unhealthy activities despite full knowledge of the risks stems mainly from undue optimism and deep-rooted addiction. Nevertheless, this problem can be successfully resolved if governments elevate taxes on harmful goods and drive intensive health campaigns.
