## 2. Segment & Sentence-by-Sentence Blueprint

### Introduction (1 Sentence)
- **Sentence 1**: *The table compares the flow of university exchange students between selected European countries and Australia in two distinct years, 2007 and 2009.*

### Body 1: Direction 1 (Europe to Australia) (3–4 Sentences)
- **Sentence 1 (Aggregate Total & Starting Leader)**: *Regarding students traveling from Europe to Australia, the reported total rose from 186 to 226, with France originally serving as the primary contributor at 61 scholars.*
- **Sentence 2 (Major Climber / Dramatic Surge)**: *By 2009, however, Germany saw its exchange numbers more than double from 27 to 63, displacing France as the largest contributor.*
- **Sentence 3 (Secondary Movements & Contrasts)**: *Similarly, figures for Sweden rose from 32 to 42, whereas France edged down from 61 to 60, while the UK fell from 31 to 25.*

### Body 2: Direction 2 (Australia to Europe) (3–4 Sentences)
- **Sentence 1 (Macro Flow Volume & Realignment)**: *In the reverse direction, although total outbound student numbers from Australia remained lower at 152 in both years, national shares repositioned noticeably.*
- **Sentence 2 (Top Destination Surge)**: *France witnessed a substantial influx, rising sharply from 37 to 58 and emerging as the most sought-after European destination by 2009.*
- **Sentence 3 (Counter-trend / Steepest Drop)**: *Conversely, while Germany posted a modest gain, the Netherlands experienced the steepest contraction, dropping from 32 to 18.*

### Overview (2 Sentences)
- **Sentence 1 (Directional Disparity)**: *Overall, the volume of students moving from Europe to Australia was significantly higher than those traveling in the opposite direction in both years.*
- **Sentence 2 (Standout Growth Performers)**: *Furthermore, Germany recorded the most dramatic surge among European sending nations, whereas France established itself as the preferred destination for Australian students.*

## 3. Recommended Vocabulary, Collocations & Phrasal Frames
- **Describing Flow & Aggregates**: *exchange numbers more than doubled*, *the flow of university exchange students*, *national shares repositioned noticeably*.
- **Rankings & Supremacy**: *displacing France as the largest contributor*, *emerging as the most sought-after European destination*, *experienced the steepest contraction*.
- **Directional Transitions**: *In the reverse direction...*, *Regarding students traveling from Europe to Australia...*

---

## 4. Critical Traps & Band-Draining Mistakes to Avoid
1. **Writing the Overview First**: In table tasks with many figures, guessing the overview upfront leads to superficial or factually incorrect claims. Process the data first, write bodies, then craft the overview.
2. **Quoting Every Single Number**: A table report is a synthesis, not a transcription. Focus on totals, maximums, minimums, and dramatic movers.

> **Source data note:** The 2007 Europe-to-Australia country entries add to 187, but the printed total is 186. The sample identifies 186 as the reported total; verify the source before using it for assessment.
