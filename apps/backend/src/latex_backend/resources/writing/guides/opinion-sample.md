## 2. Model answer (teaching example)

Providing higher education free of charge to all students has been advocated as a transformative policy to promote social equity and workforce development. While I acknowledge that removing financial barriers enables underprivileged individuals to access tertiary education, I partially disagree with this assertion because an unconditional universal guarantee would impose an untenable burden on government coffers, necessitating a targeted rather than blanket approach.

On the one hand, advocating for tuition-free university education is firmly grounded in the necessity to democratize academic opportunities. In the contemporary economic environment, escalating inflation coupled with the rising costs of university administration has made tertiary education increasingly inaccessible for students from disadvantaged socioeconomic backgrounds. When talented youths are prevented from pursuing degrees due to financial hardship, the national workforce is deprived of vital skilled human resources, ultimately hindering broader economic expansion. If governments absorb educational fees for these scholars, exceptional candidates can contribute meaningfully to the knowledge economy, thereby elevating productivity and standard of living for the entire populace.

On the other hand, the proposition of universal free higher education possesses an idealistic nature that overlooks practical macroeconomic constraints. For a policy of universal tuition coverage to be sustainable, three prerequisites must simultaneously exist: a moderate student population, modest university operating expenditures, and a substantial national budget surplus. In reality, modern economies feature massive student cohorts, specialized laboratories require enormous capital investment, and state budgets already confront persistent deficits. Imposing an absolute financial obligation on the government to fund every student would inevitably overstretch public finances, risking a deterioration in educational standards or forcing austerity upon other critical public sectors like healthcare and infrastructure.

In conclusion, while subsidizing university tuition is indispensable for empowering capable but underprivileged students, universal free provision represents an impractical drain on national financial reserves. Governments should therefore allocate financial assistance on a case-by-case basis, fully subsidizing disadvantaged applicants while expecting self-sufficient households to fulfill their educational financial obligations.

## 3. Sentence-by-Sentence Breakdown & Analysis
- **Introduction**: Employs nominalization (*Providing higher education free of charge...*) and sets out a clear, balanced stance (*partially disagree... untenable burden... targeted rather than blanket approach*).
- **Body 1 (Merit)**: Broad topic sentence; highlights real-world inflation and admin expenses; links exclusion to workforce depletion; details the economic payoff.
- **Body 2 (Limitation)**: Identifies the *idealistic nature*; defines the 3 Preconditions (cohort volume, operational expenditure, budget surpluses); contrasts with persistent modern deficits; projects risk of austerity or quality erosion.
- **Conclusion**: Resolves the tension through a means-tested compromise (*case-by-case basis*, *fulfill their educational financial obligations*).
