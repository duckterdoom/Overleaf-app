# IELTS Writing Task 2: Agree / Disagree — Master Guide & Template (v2)

## 1. Topic Architecture & Argument Diagram

*Figure 1: Conceptual Argument Architecture — Free Higher Education Balanced Evaluation*

- **Question Core**: "To what extent do you agree or disagree".
- **Huy Forum 9.0 Strategy**: Avoid forcing 100% agreement. When you force 100%, you must generate two completely separate sets of benefits, which frequently leads to idea repetition and circular logic. A **partial agreement (balanced approach)** creates a nuanced argument grounded in real-world policy constraints.

## 2. Segment & Sentence-by-Sentence Blueprint

### Introduction (2 Sentences)
- **Sentence 1 (Paraphrase via Nominalization)**: Turn the verb "provide" into the gerund/noun phrase "Providing higher education free of charge...".
  - *Providing higher education free of charge to all students has been advocated as a necessary measure to promote social mobility.*
- **Sentence 2 (Thesis of Partial Agreement)**: Express conditional endorsement.
  - *While I acknowledge the profound socioeconomic benefits of removing financial barriers for disadvantaged learners, I partially disagree with this proposal due to the immense fiscal strain it imposes on national budgets, warranting a more targeted approach.*

### Body 1: The Justification / Societal Upside (4–5 Sentences)
- **Sentence 1 (Broad Topic Sentence)**: Establish the rationale without boxing yourself in.
  - *On the one hand, advocating for free tertiary education is firmly grounded in the necessity to democratize human capital.*
- **Sentence 2 (Real-World Economic Bottleneck)**: Describe current reality (inflation, tuition inflation, financial burden).
  - *In the contemporary economic landscape, escalating inflation and surging institutional operational costs have made university degrees increasingly unaffordable for individuals from disadvantaged socioeconomic backgrounds.*
- **Sentence 3 (Consequence of Inaction)**: Explain how this locks talent out of the workforce.
  - *When talented students are deterred by substantial tuition fees, national workforces are deprived of vital intellectual resources, perpetuating generational inequality and curtailing economic development.*
- **Sentence 4 (Mechanism of State Support)**: Explain the transformative effect of free access.
  - *Under conditions where governments absorb educational expenses, gifted youths can fulfill their potential without the threat of insurmountable debt, ultimately enhancing national productivity and standard of living across society.*

### Body 2: Practical Limitations & Fiscal Constraints (4–5 Sentences)
- **Sentence 1 (Topic Sentence - Idealistic Nature)**: State that despite its moral appeal, the proposal is financially untenable as a blanket policy.
  - *On the other hand, the proposition of universal tuition coverage exhibits an overly idealistic nature that overlooks critical macroeconomic constraints.*
- **Sentence 2 (The Three Preconditions Formula)**: Introduce the fiscal reality test.
  - *For a government to realistically fund higher education for all citizens, three prerequisites must align: a manageable student population, modest university expenditure, and an expansive national budget surplus.*
- **Sentence 3 (The Actual Fiscal Reality)**: Contrast prerequisites with modern reality.
  - *However, in most modern economies, tertiary enrollment is extensive, advanced academic facilities demand exorbitant operational capital, and state coffers already face persistent budgetary deficits.*
- **Sentence 4 (Failure Consequence)**: Explain what happens if blanket funding is forced.
  - *Under such circumstances, an absolute financial guarantee would inevitably overburden government finances, compelling universities to compromise on educational quality or leading to severe cuts in other vital public services.*

### Conclusion (2 Sentences)
- **Sentence 1 (Summary of Core Tension)**:
  - *In conclusion, while eliminating tuition fees is instrumental in supporting underprivileged students and cultivating talent, universal coverage represents an impractical burden on state treasuries.*
- **Sentence 2 (Pragmatic Policy Solution)**:
  - *Governments should therefore implement financial assistance on a case-by-case basis, subsidizing those in genuine need while requiring affluent households to fulfill their educational financial obligations.*

## 3. Recommended Vocabulary, Collocations & Phrasal Frames
- **Nominalization & Stance**: *providing higher education*, *democratize human capital*, *partially disagree with this proposal*, *grounded in the necessity to...*
- **Socioeconomic Terms**: *disadvantaged socioeconomic backgrounds*, *generational inequality*, *national workforces are deprived of vital intellectual resources*, *insurmountable debt*.
- **Fiscal Constraint Collocations**: *idealistic nature*, *government coffers*, *budget deficit*, *exorbitant operational capital*, *overburden government finances*, *case-by-case basis*, *fulfill their financial obligations*.

---

## 4. Critical Traps & Band-Draining Mistakes to Avoid
1. **The 100% Agreement Echo Trap**: Saying "it helps poor students" in Body 1 and "it creates more jobs" in Body 2 is essentially repeating the same human capital point. A balanced approach avoids this entirely.
2. **Writing Overly Narrow Topic Sentences**: Keep the topic sentence broad (*grounded in the necessity to democratize human capital*).
3. **Moralizing Without Grounding**: Simply asserting "education is a human right and must be free" scores low on Task Response. You must ground the claim in economic mechanics (inflation, tuition cost, cohort size, budget deficits).
