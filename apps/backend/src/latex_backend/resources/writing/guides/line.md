# IELTS Writing Task 1: Line Graph — Master Guide & Template (v2)

## 1. Visual Prompt & Topic Framing

![Healthcare Satisfaction Line Graph](/api/writing/guide-assets/e7ba349cda282a3b36889072fe19c077098023685ff83b26d3b23585daab9357.jpg)
*Figure 1: Percentage of people satisfied with the healthcare system in 4 countries (1991–2007)*

- **Visual Capture**: Dynamic trends over a chronological timeline (years/months).
- **Core Mental Model**: Avoid describing data line-by-line or point-by-point. Split the graph into **two distinct tiers** (e.g., Higher Tier vs. Lower Tier, or Net Gainers vs. Net Losers).

---

## 2. Segment & Sentence-by-Sentence Blueprint

### Introduction (1 Sentence)
- **Segment Mission**: Paraphrase the prompt completely using noun phrases and clear prepositional frames.
- **Sentence 1**: *The line graph illustrates the proportion of citizens satisfied with their national healthcare system in four different countries between 1991 and 2007, measured in percentages.*

### Overview (2 Sentences)
- **Segment Mission**: Summarize macroscopic trends and the most prominent difference without quoting raw statistics.
- **Sentence 1 (Dominance / Overall Winner)**: Identify which line maintained supremacy or finished at the absolute highest level, and which remained at the bottom.
  - *Overall, while the Netherlands recorded the most consistently high satisfaction figures after overtaking Canada in the late 1990s, the US remained the lowest throughout by a wide margin.*
- **Sentence 2 (Trajectory / Volatility Contrast)**: Contrast the nature of movement (e.g., sharp U-shaped swing vs. stable gradual climb).
  - *In addition, Canada underwent the most pronounced fluctuation, whereas the trajectories for the UK and the US were characterized by modest upward increments.*

### Body 1: The Dominant / Higher Tier (3–4 Sentences)
- **Segment Mission**: Analyze the leading entities, focusing on starting levels, major pivot points, and concluding standings.
- **Sentence 1 (Baseline Leader & Turning Point)**: State starting baseline of the primary leader and its initial trajectory.
  - *Looking first at the higher figures, Canada commenced at just under 60%, making it the most satisfied nation initially, although this metric plummeted to a trough of roughly 18% in 1999.*
- **Sentence 2 (Recovery / Rebound)**: Describe the secondary movement and concluding position.
  - *Thereafter, this figure rebounded noticeably to close at 39% in 2007.*
- **Sentence 3 (Stable Competitor & Overtaking)**: Introduce the steadier competitor that surpassed the volatile leader.
  - *By contrast, the Netherlands displayed a far steadier path, rising from about 48% to 49% before easing to roughly 46%, still the highest rate in 2007.*

### Body 2: The Secondary / Lower Tier (3–4 Sentences)
- **Segment Mission**: Synthesize lower-ranking entities, highlighting shared traits and comparative gaps.
- **Sentence 1 (Lower Baseline & Gradual Increase)**: Describe the higher of the two lower figures.
  - *Turning to the remaining nations, the UK began at approximately 23% before progressing steadily to nearly 30% by 2007.*
- **Sentence 2 (Weaker Parallel Trajectory)**: Describe the lowest line, emphasizing its subdued momentum.
  - *A similar, albeit noticeably flatter, trajectory was observed in the US, which merely edged up from 10% to 11% and settled at 12%.*
- **Sentence 3 (Comparative Synthesis)**: Connect both tiers to reinforce the vast gap between upper and lower tiers.
  - *In other words, despite improvements in both lower-tier nations, neither came close to matching the satisfaction levels recorded in the upper bracket.*

## 3. Recommended Vocabulary, Collocations & Phrasal Frames
- **Opening Tier Transitions**: *Looking first at the higher figures...*, *Turning to the remaining categories...*
- **Describing Extremes & Standings**: *making it the most satisfied group initially*, *eclipsed [Entity]*, *by a wide margin*.
- **Fluctuations & Reversals**: *plummeted to an all-time low of...*, *rebounded noticeably to close at...*, *narrower band of fluctuation*.
- **Subdued Trends**: *followed a similar, albeit flatter, trajectory*, *edged up marginally*, *neither came close to matching the levels witnessed in...*

---

## 4. Critical Traps & Band-Draining Mistakes to Avoid
1. **The Crossing Line Trap**: Do not split Body 1 and Body 2 based on lines that cross each other randomly. Group by starting or overall volume brackets.
2. **Repeating Units Obsessively**: Mention percent or units once at the start of the clause rather than attaching `%` to every single number.
3. **Omitting the Overview's Relative Difference**: An overview that only mentions "all lines increased" without noting the dominant leader misses Band 7+ Task Achievement criteria.
